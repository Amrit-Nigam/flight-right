# What Flight Right can and cannot claim (plan Phase 8.3)

Every number below names the file it comes from, so it can be checked rather than trusted.
When a number here and a number in a source file disagree, the source file wins, and this
document is wrong and must be fixed. Last reviewed 25 September 2026.

---

## Can claim, with numbers

**Conflict detection**

- **The classifier catches 94.0% of conflicts with at least three minutes of warning:** 267 of
  284 on the held-out test pairs, 95% interval 90.6–96.2%. Source: `docs/metrics.json`
  (`model2.headline_three_minute_warning.xgboost`); interval from `docs/ablation.json`.
- **The running system catches 94.7%:** 269 of 284, 95% interval 91.5–96.8%, at 101.1 false
  alarms per 1,000 pairs. Source: `ml_pipeline/reports/offsets_ab_20260924_181140.json`
  (`per_mode rule_only`). The two intervals overlap almost entirely, so never present the
  difference between them as a gain.
- **The classifier beats the geometric rule at the rule's own false-alarm cost:** at 73.8
  false alarms per 1,000 pairs, 93.7% (266 of 284) against 87.3% (248). Source:
  `ml_pipeline/reports/reevaluation_20260923_095646.json` (`model2.at_rule_false_alarm_cost`).
  The 93.3% in older text is the 5 September model's figure.

**Trajectory prediction** (ten minutes ahead, held-out test split; source `docs/ablation.md`,
generated by `ml_pipeline/utils/ablation.py`)

- **Each change's contribution, one row at a time,** including the two that were measured and
  not adopted (candidate pruning and the archive data).
- **The served model's single best guess beats the non-neural tree reference at ten
  minutes:** 44.2% against 40.5% for arrivals, and 66.7% against 65.7% for all traffic. Until the
  per-mode offsets arrived, the tree had beaten every neural model on every split.
- **"One of five candidate paths within 5 NM" for arrivals is 72.0% ± 0.4 across four
  seeds** (served run 71.5%). **Within 5 NM and 1,000 ft, the measure that matters for
  conflicts, it is 44.1%.** Quote both, and label the first as coverage.
- **The per-mode switch halved the ten-minute height error:** 2,231 ft down to 1,116 ft. Source:
  `ml_pipeline/reports/multipath_3d_20260925_030155.json`.

**The cost of checking several candidate paths, measured both ways**

- **Letting the model's paths trigger the safety net added 86–111 false alerts for 0–2 real
  conflicts,** and dropped one real conflict whose straight-line prediction breached the
  minimum. It was fixed on 24 September. Source: `offsets_ab_20260924_181140.json`.
- **Pruning the candidate paths cuts coverage** (arrivals best-of-5 71.5% → 66.0%) and does not
  help alerting. Source: `docs/ablation.md`.

**What hearing the radio would be worth (UPPER BOUND, assuming perfect clearance recognition)**

- **Arrivals, one of five within 5 NM and 1,000 ft: 43.4% → 53.8%**, and the single best guess
  within 5 NM: 42.8% → 45.1%. Label it as an upper bound every time. Source:
  `ml_pipeline/reports/oracle_clearance_20260925_031547.json`, `docs/ablation.md`.
- **Level clearances help when used to cap altitude (EUROCONTROL STCA Option E), and hurt when
  used to eliminate paths:** arrivals best-of-5 fell 70.4% → 64.8%, and the closest path was
  thrown away 9.2% of the time. That is a measured design finding.

**Data**

- **OpenSky archive coverage of Indian airspace at low altitude begins in January 2025.** Nine
  sampled months in 2023–24 have zero states below 10,000 ft near VABB. Source:
  `docs/phase2_findings.md`, 2.3.
- **adsb.lol per-aircraft history can be fetched directly for a rolling six weeks;** older days
  are patchy. Source: `ml_pipeline/reports/adsblol_history_20260924_232209.json`.
- **12.5% of the public ATCO2 test set is the same audio as the training data of the published
  `whisper-large-v3-atco2-asr-atcosim` model.** Found by waveform correlation. Source:
  `docs/phase2_findings.md`, 2.5.

- **Autopilot selected-altitude coverage over Mumbai (plan 9.0), which to our knowledge has not
  been published for Indian airspace:** 99.6% of 4,090 aircraft-days (24 Aug–5 Sep 2026) broadcast
  it; 94.8% of airborne reports within 60 NM of VABB carry it. Source:
  `ml_pipeline/reports/adsblol_coverage_20260925_042546.json`. Data: adsb.lol, ODbL 1.0 (credit it).
- **Free callsign-route lookups for Indian flights (plan 9.2):** adsbdb gets the route exactly
  right for 49.0% of 1,923 flights (69.1% of Mumbai flights); hexdb for 19.0%. Truth is OpenSky
  `flights_data4`, which agrees with our own detected VABB landings 96.3% of the time. Source:
  `ml_pipeline/reports/route_lookup_*.json`.

- **Near Mumbai, the dialled altitude is usually a step, not the destination:** of aircraft
  changing level, 20.5% have been cleared past it within 3 minutes and 77.5% within 10. So it is
  useful for the next minute or two, not for ten-minute prediction. Source: Section 14 of
  `docs/CLAUDE.md`, 25 Sep.
- **The dialled altitude makes the conflict classifier better at the same false-alarm cost**
  in all four seeds tested. Source: `docs/ablation.md`,
  `ml_pipeline/reports/classifier_intent_20260925_050817.json`. Not served; do not claim it is live.

## Cannot claim

- **Any speech-to-text, callsign-matching or clearance-extraction accuracy.** The speech chain
  (plan Phases 4–6) has not been built. Only the oracle upper bound exists.
- **That voice improves trajectory prediction.** Only the upper bound is measured, and the live
  proof (`ml_pipeline/reports/clearance_demo_*.json`) shows what an oracle clearance actually
  changes on the running system, which is less than the upper bound suggests.
- **Any figure on real Indian ATC radio, or on Indian-accented phraseology.** No recordings
  exist yet (plan Phase 5, needs classmates).
- **Any ATCO2 figure from `whisper-large-v3-atco2-asr-atcosim` as held out,** because of the
  12.5% overlap.
- **The published 87–95% or 74.2% callsign-accuracy figures, or the SIA-FTP robustness
  result.** Different recognisers.
- **"71.5% accurate" or "72% accurate".** Those figures are best-of-five coverage and
  horizontal only. The single best guess is 44.2%; best-of-five in 3-D is 44.1%.
- **That the archive data improves the system.** It improves trajectory coverage (75.6% ± 0.6),
  but through the alert chain it cost one real tier 1 alarm on validation and was not served.
- **That the LSTM is the best trajectory model.** The tree beats it, and the per-mode
  multi-path model beats the tree.
- **">95% accuracy" as a blanket claim.** It is defensible at one and three minutes, and
  nowhere near that at ten.
- **Production readiness, certification, or real-time transcription.**

## Must state

- **Four critical runtime defects were found by driving the running system, not by tests**
  (`docs/ISSUES.md` C3, C5 and the 8 Sep replay defects).
- **The classifier was computed but ignored by the alarm decision until 22 September.** The
  running system caught 87.3% before, and catches 94.7% now.
- **The secondary-conflict check ran on an empty traffic list in production until 22 September.**
- **Until 24 September the published running-system figure (94.7%) omitted a step:** the
  served system actually caught 94.4%, because the safety net was reading the model's
  worst-case path.
- **Until 25 September the multi-path model's altitude had never been scored.** The old
  version was no better than a straight line at height.
- **Seven real conflicts on the test split sit in the collapsed low-confidence group** with
  more than three minutes to go. They are still on the active list, but not shown prominently
  (`docs/ISSUES.md` H5, a decision for Archit).
- **Manoeuvre-advice defects C1 and C2 are still open,** and so is the audit gap H1 (most
  audit entries never leave the browser).
- **GATE 1 (classifier recall ≥ 0.95) still fails at 0.9439.** The recall-margin decision is
  Archit's and has not been made.
